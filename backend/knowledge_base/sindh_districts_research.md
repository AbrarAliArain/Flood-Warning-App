              Sindh Flood-Prone Districts

Research Report: Flood History, Drainage & Elevation Risk (2022-2026)

For Aqua Shield -- Hackathon Research Document

Districts covered: Hyderabad, Dadu, Jamshoro, Matiari, Tando Allahyar, Tando Muhammad Khan,
Shaheed Benazirabad, Naushahro Feroze, Sanghar.

Quick Summary (In Plain Words)

Yes -- all 9 districts have flooded before, and most of them flood almost every time there is heavy rain.
The two main reasons are: (1) they sit on very flat, low land close to the Indus River or Manchar Lake, and
(2) the drainage system built to protect them (called LBOD) no longer works well against today's heavier
rains.

District-by-District Details

1. Hyderabad
· Floods almost every monsoon due to poor city drainage, not just river overflow -- rapid urban growth

    increased paved surfaces, which increases rainwater runoff and overwhelms the stormwater drainage
    system.

· Has repeatedly suffered urban flooding in 2001, 2003, 2008, 2016, and 2017.
· In August 2025, disaster authorities specifically warned that 50-100mm of rain in a short time could

    cause urban flooding in Hyderabad -- showing this risk is still active now.
· 2022 damage: 19,556 houses.

2. Dadu
· One of the worst-hit districts in Pakistan. Home to Manchar Lake, Pakistan's largest lake.
· In 2022, officials deliberately breached the lake's banks to stop a bigger overflow into cities, but this still

    flooded around 135,000 people living in smaller nearby villages.

· A drainage canal near Dadu (Main Nara Valley Drain) also broke apart naturally under water pressure in

    2022.

· Still flagged as high-risk in 2025 -- disaster authorities warned Dadu is at risk of sudden flooding due to

    rising Indus River water levels.

· 2022 damage: 156,210 houses -- the highest of any district on this list.

3. Jamshoro
· Shares Manchar Lake with Dadu, and also receives fast-moving runoff water flowing down from the

    mountains of Balochistan onto its flat land.

· Flagged again in the 2025 monsoon season for sudden flooding risk due to rising river levels.
· 2022 damage: 55,401 houses.

4. Matiari
· Sits directly along the Indus River. In 2022, flooding here was mainly caused by the Indus River

    overflowing straight into the floodplain, not just rainfall.

· 2022 damage: 45,879 houses.

5. Tando Allahyar
· Flat, low-lying land. Flagged for urban flooding risk again in the 2025 monsoon season.
· 2022 damage: 29,971 houses.

6. Tando Muhammad Khan
· Low-lying district. In 2025 monsoon reports, this area experienced nearly 20 hours of continuous rainfall

    causing accumulated flooding.

· 2022 damage: 28,456 houses.

7. Shaheed Benazirabad (Nawabshah)
· Very flat terrain -- intense rainfall over prolonged periods on flat land was specifically observed causing

    flooding here after the 2022 floods.

· One of three districts (with Sanghar and Mirpurkhas) that a major drainage project called LBOD was

    built specifically to protect -- but the drainage system is now considered faulty and unable to handle
    heavy rain properly.

· 2022 damage: 113,590 houses.

8. Naushahro Feroze
· One of the hardest-hit districts -- floodwater remained stagnant here for months after the 2022 floods,

    still present as of January 2023.

· 2022 damage: 134,960 houses -- second highest on this list.

9. Sanghar
· Also part of the LBOD drainage zone meant to protect it, but which is now considered outdated and

    unable to cope with heavy rainfall.

· 2022 damage: 104,830 houses.

The Big Pattern Across All 9 Districts

· Elevation: Most of these districts sit on very flat, low-lying land close to the Indus River or Manchar

    Lake -- so when the river rises or the lake overflows, water has nowhere to drain naturally.

· Drainage: The main drainage system for the middle part of Sindh (LBOD) is described by experts as

    unable to cope with climate-driven heavy rainfall anymore.

· Recurring problem, not a one-time event: Fresh flood warnings were issued again for Dadu,

    Jamshoro, and urban flooding risk in Hyderabad in August 2025 -- meaning the risk is active right up to
    this year, not just history from 2022.

What This Means for Your Project

These 9 districts are strong candidates to add to Aqua Shield's monitoring list because they combine three
risk factors at once: low elevation near a major river/lake, outdated or overwhelmed drainage infrastructure,
and a proven, repeated flooding history from 2022 all the way through 2025. Highlighting Dadu, Jamshoro,
and Naushahro Feroze as your lead examples in a pitch would be strongest, since they have the highest
damage numbers and the clearest documented causes (Manchar Lake, Indus overflow, and prolonged
stagnant flooding).
